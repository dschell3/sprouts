    How to play
    1. The garden starts with a few seeds. Player 1 goes first.
    2. On your turn, drag a vine from one seed to another seed. Or loop it back to the same seed.
    3. A new seed sprouts in the middle of your vine.
    4. Vines can't cross each other or themselves. They can't pass through a seed.
    5. A seed can hold at most 3 vines. A loop counts as 2.
    6. If you can't grow a vine on your turn, you lose. The last player to move wins.
    Auto vs Challenge
    • Auto: the game spots the end for you and names the winner.
    • Challenge: the game never tells you it's over. When you think you have no move, tap "I'm stuck". The game checks your claim.
    For the curious (spoilers)
    • Each seed starts with 3 free shoots. A move uses 2 shoots, and the new seed brings 1. So every move removes exactly 1 shoot from the garden.
    • That means a game with n seeds lasts at most 3n − 1 moves. Why not 3n? Look at the last seed that sprouts.
    • On the flat square, a game also always lasts at least 2n moves.
    • On the flat square, with perfect play, Player 1 seems to win exactly when n leaves a remainder of 3, 4 or 5 when divided by 6. Computers have checked this for many values of n, but nobody has proved it yet.
    • On the torus and the Klein bottle, a loop around the surface fences nothing off. Both sides of it are the same patch of soil. Does that change who wins?
